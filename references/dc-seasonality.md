# DC seasonality data

Primary source: DC Farm to School / DC Greens / OSSE "What's Growing Around Here?" seasonality chart (2013). The chart is pictures only, so the month-by-month table below was read from the images. It covers about 35 common crops. Crops the chart doesn't show are covered in "Supplemental crops" further down, drawn from other regional sources. Cross-checked against the Virginia Grown availability calendar (VDACS), the Maryland's Best seasonality chart, and FRESHFARM's Mid-Atlantic guide.

Each month lists what the chart shows growing near Washington, DC. **Feature** is the chart's "Farm-Fresh Feature" for that month, the crop at its peak.

**Dec-Feb (storage months):** too cold for anything to grow. The chart lists what keeps well in a cool, dry place from the fall harvest: apples, pears, potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots, beets, butternut squash, acorn squash. Treat these as "local, stored" rather than "fresh in season".

## Shoulder-month rule of thumb

A crop is fully in season in the months listed and at its best in its feature month. Treat the month before and after a listed range as "early/late, probably fine at a farmers market"; treat anything else as out of season.

## Cross-check against other regional sources

Where the sources agree, the chart data above is solid: asparagus (Apr-Jun), strawberries (Apr-Jun), okra and eggplant (Jul-Sep), green beans (Jun-Sep/Oct), peppers (Jul-Oct), summer squash (Jun-Oct), winter squash (Aug/Sep-Nov), sweet potatoes (Sep-Dec, then stored), kale/chard/spinach/radishes in late spring and again in fall, and broccoli/cauliflower/Brussels-type brassicas in fall.

Where they differ, apply these adjustments:

- **Spring brassicas:** Virginia and Maryland put broccoli and cauliflower at roughly May-Jun and Oct-Nov; the DC chart also shows them in Mar-Apr. Treat Mar-Apr broccoli, cauliflower and cabbage as "early or protected-crop, possible but not the best bet"; May-Jun and Sep-Nov are the reliable windows.
- **Corn:** the chart shows Aug-Sep; Virginia and Maryland start in Jun-Jul. Treat Jul as early corn.
- **Potatoes:** the chart shows Sep-Nov (plus storage). Virginia and Maryland also show new potatoes Jun-Sep, so treat summer potatoes as fine.
- **Cabbage:** also available Jun-Jul in Virginia and Maryland, on top of the chart's Mar-Apr and Sep-Nov.
- **Peas and asparagus:** peas run May-Jul and again Sep-Oct; asparagus runs to Jun.
- **Kale and chard:** a real summer gap (Jul-Aug) is normal because of heat. Chard is back Sep-Nov; collards run Jun-Nov as a heat-tolerant green.
- **Fall spinach:** Sep-Nov in Maryland and FRESHFARM, in addition to the chart's spring appearance.
- **Fruit:** Virginia has peaches and nectarines Jul-Aug, blackberries/blueberries Jun-Aug, raspberries Jun-Jul and again Aug-Oct, grapes Aug-Oct, watermelon Jul-Sep. That's slightly narrower than the chart for summer fruit; treat Jul-Aug as the safest window for peaches and berries.
- **Tomatoes:** Virginia shows Jul-Oct; the chart's Jul feature and Aug-Sep listings agree. (The Maryland chart's tomato dots looked mis-plotted, so it wasn't used for them.)
